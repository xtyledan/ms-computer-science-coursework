## Module Overview

Prompt engineering is the practice of designing effective inputs, or **prompts**, that guide artificial intelligence models toward producing accurate, relevant, and high-quality responses. Rather than simply asking a question, prompt engineering focuses on providing the right context, instructions, and constraints to improve the AI's output. Well-crafted prompts are especially important in software development, where AI can assist with writing code, debugging, documentation, testing, and problem solving.

As AI systems become more advanced, prompt engineering has evolved from trial and error into a structured discipline. Several frameworks have been developed to help users consistently create effective prompts for different tasks and applications.

One commonly used framework is **SIMPLE** (Situation, Intent, Mechanics, Phrasing, Limitations, Evaluation), which emphasizes clear objectives, specific instructions, and continuous refinement. Another is **SCREAM** (Structure, Context, Role, Example, Audience, Mechanics), which focuses on organizing prompts so AI models better understand the intended task and audience.

Additional techniques such as **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting** encourage AI models to reason through problems step by step, improving performance on complex coding and analytical tasks. **Self-refinement** and **iterative prompting** further improve response quality by allowing AI to evaluate and revise its own outputs through structured feedback.

Research has shown that structured prompt engineering techniques can significantly improve the accuracy, consistency, and usefulness of AI-generated responses, particularly for programming, technical writing, and multi-step reasoning tasks. As AI becomes increasingly integrated into software engineering workflows, understanding how to communicate effectively with AI tools has become an essential technical skill.
